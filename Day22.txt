Repeater helps to increase the signal strength 

A repeater is a fundamental networking device that operates at the Physical Layer (OSI Layer 1) and is used to extend the range of a network
 by regenerating weak or distorted signals. As data travels over long distances through wired or wireless media, it loses strength due to 
 attenuation, noise, interference, and timing issues. This leads to signal degradation and potential data errors.

To solve this, a repeater receives the weakened signal, cleans and regenerates it, and then retransmits it with restored strength. 
Importantly, it does not analyze data, frames, or addresses, and it does not make routing or switching decisions. Its only function
 is to ensure that the signal remains strong enough to continue traveling across the network.

Repeaters are used in both wired networks (like Ethernet and fiber optics) and wireless systems (like Wi-Fi and cellular communication). 
In digital systems, modern repeaters perform 3R functions: reamplify, reshape, and retime, which helps remove noise and restore signal
 quality. 
 
 In optical fiber networks, devices like optical amplifiers (EDFA) and OEO regenerators are used for long-distance communication,
  while wireless repeaters help eliminate coverage dead zones.

Different types of repeaters exist based on signal type (analog or digital), connection type (wired or wireless), location (local or remote),
 and technology (microwave, optical, radio, and telephone repeaters). Earlier networks also used hubs as multiport repeaters, but they are 
 now largely replaced by switches due to better efficiency.

The main advantages of repeaters include extended network range, improved signal quality, low cost, and simple deployment. However,
they also have limitations such as no traffic control, inability to segment networks, potential increase in noise, and collision
 issues in older systems.

In modern networking, repeaters are still essential in fiber backbone infrastructure, wireless coverage extension, industrial systems, 
and long-distance communication links, even though they are often combined with more advanced devices like switches and routers.

A repeater acts like a signal booster that strengthens weak communication signals so data can travel longer distances reliably without 
degradation.
