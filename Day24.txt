Today we will discuss about Hub

A Hub is a hardware networking device used to connect multiple devices in a Local Area Network (LAN).
It Operates at the Physical Layer (Layer 1) of the OSI model 
It is also known as  multi-port repeater
 Broadcasts data to all connected devices 
It does not filter data
 Uses shared bandwidth
It contains the multiple port , internal circuitry and single repeater mechanism 

Working of Hub 
A sender device sends data to the hub.
 Hub receives the signal. 
Hub broadcasts the signal to all ports.
 Every connected device receives the data. 
Only the intended device accepts the frame.

Sender   --->    Hub     --   Receiver 
Hub cannot identify destination addresses.

When two devices send data simultaneously, signals collide and is known as Collision 

In Hub:
Entire network shares one collision domain 
Data may be destroyed 
Devices send a Jam Signal 
Devices wait random time before retransmission 

It results in slower communication and reduced efficiency 

A passive hub only connects devices.
Features : 
No signal amplification 
No signal regeneration 
No electricity required 
Simply forwards signals 

It is cheap and simple 
Weak signal over long distance

Active Hub
An active hub regenerates and amplifies signals.
Requires power supply 
Strengthens weak signals 
Improves communication quality 

Better signal transmission & longer communication distance 
More expensive

Intelligent Hub
A smart hub with management capabilities.
Traffic monitoring 
Troubleshooting support 
Port management 
Contains MIB (Management Information Base) 

It has Better network control and Easy fault detection 
It has Higher cost 
Also it is more complex

Hub Affect Network Speed
Shared bandwidth among all devices
 Broadcasts data to every port 
Increased collisions
 More traffic congestion

Slower communication
 Delays in data transmission
Hub is used in Small LAN , Schools and labs
Hub is mostly replaced by switches due to better performance.
